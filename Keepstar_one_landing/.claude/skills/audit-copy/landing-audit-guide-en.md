# Guide: Landing Page Copy Audit (Infostyle Method)

> Instructions for an LLM agent. Go through every section of the landing page and check the copy against the checklists below. For every issue found: quote the original, explain the problem, and suggest a revised version.

---

## How to Use This Guide

1. Get the landing page text (URL, screenshot, or copy-paste).
2. Break the page into semantic blocks: header, hero section, benefits, how it works, testimonials, CTA, footer.
3. Run each block through **all 7 checklists** below.
4. For every issue, output in this format:

```
🔴 Block: [block name]
Issue: [brief description]
Before: "...original text..."
After: "...suggested revision..."
Why: [explanation referencing the principle]
```

5. Finish with a summary: number of issues per category + overall assessment.

---

## 1. Value Judgments → Facts and Specifics

**Principle:** Readers don't trust other people's opinions. "Best," "unique," "fast" — these are the author's judgments, not facts. Replace every judgment with concrete data so the reader can draw their own conclusion.

### Checklist

- [ ] **No empty adjectives.** Look for: "best," "unique," "effective," "high-quality," "innovative," "cutting-edge," "world-class," "reliable," "professional," "comprehensive," "optimal," "seamless." Each one is a candidate for replacement with a fact.
- [ ] **No unsubstantiated claims.** If the copy says "fast delivery," it should specify how fast (e.g., "delivered within 2 hours in the metro area"). If "affordable prices," there should be an actual price or comparison.
- [ ] **No empty superlatives.** "The most," "the best," "#1" — without a source or methodology, these are hollow.
- [ ] **No vague quantities.** "Many clients" → "over 3,000 clients since 2019." "Wide selection" → "427 items in the catalog."
- [ ] **Every claim is verifiable.** If you remove an adjective and the meaning survives — the adjective was empty.

### Replacement Examples

| Value Judgment | Fact |
|---|---|
| Fast delivery | Delivered within 2 hours inside the city limits |
| Affordable prices | Average order: $45, free shipping over $100 |
| Wide selection | 427 furniture items: from stools to full kitchen sets |
| Team of professionals | 12 engineers, average 8 years of experience, 4 patents |
| High quality | 5-year warranty, 0.3% return rate |

## 2. Headlines: Descriptive → Transitive

**Principle:** A descriptive headline answers "what is this section about" ("Our Advantages"). A transitive headline answers "what exactly is being said here" — and works as a standalone message. You should be able to send a transitive headline to a friend in a chat and they'd understand the point without any context.

### Checklist

- [ ] **No descriptive/label headlines.** Look for: "About Us," "Our Advantages," "How It Works," "Testimonials," "Contact," "Services," "Features," "Why Choose Us." These are label headlines that carry no meaning.
- [ ] **Every headline contains a benefit or a fact.** "Our Advantages" → "Next-day delivery and no-questions-asked refunds."
- [ ] **Each headline reads autonomously.** If you pull it out of the page context, it should still make sense.
- [ ] **Subheadlines form a coherent story.** Read all subheadlines on the page in sequence: they should tell a connected narrative.
- [ ] **The main headline (h1) is transitive.** It should immediately convey what the product does and for whom. Not "A Platform for Business," but "Automate your paperwork: from invoice to signed contract in 2 minutes."

### Replacement Examples

| Descriptive | Transitive |
|---|---|
| About Us | Operating since 2015, 300+ projects delivered for retail brands |
| Our Advantages | Delivered tomorrow, installed for free, 5-year warranty |
| How It Works | Three steps from request to finished website in 2 weeks |
| Testimonials | 94% of clients recommend us to their colleagues |
| Services | We design, build, and maintain ventilation systems |

## 3. Sensory Experience Instead of Abstractions

**Principle:** Sensory experience is anything you can see, hear, touch, smell, or taste. Abstractions ("process optimization," "efficiency improvement") don't create images in the reader's mind. Good copy is anchored in reality — the reader sees a picture.

### Checklist

- [ ] **No abstract product descriptions.** "A cloud-based platform for team collaboration management" says nothing. Rewrite through the actions of a specific person: "Assign tasks to your team, see who's working on what, get alerts when deadlines are at risk."
- [ ] **Key phrases create mental images.** Read each sentence and ask: "Can I mentally picture this?" If not, the text is abstract.
- [ ] **There are real-life scenarios.** Instead of "we solve business problems," describe a situation: "Your accountant Sarah used to spend every Friday reconciling invoices. Now the system does it in 10 minutes, and Sarah leaves on time."
- [ ] **No specialist jargon for a general audience.** If the landing page is for small business owners, don't write "B2B SaaS solution for sales funnel automation." Write: "Software that automatically reminds your sales reps to follow up with leads."
- [ ] **Abstractions are paired with examples.** If an abstraction is necessary, it must be accompanied by a concrete example or scenario right next to it.

### The Test

Read the copy out loud. If it sounds like a pitch deck for investors — rewrite it as an explanation to a friend.

## 4. Benefits Over Features (The Utility Formula)

**Principle:** A feature is a product characteristic (what it does). A benefit is the outcome for the user (what it gives them). The reader doesn't care that you have "128 GB of RAM." They care that they "can run Figma, Chrome with 50 tabs, and Slack at the same time — and nothing lags."

### Checklist

- [ ] **Every feature is translated into a benefit.** Go through each item in the "advantages" section. If it states a feature, append "...which means you [get this result]."
- [ ] **Benefits are specific.** Not "saves time," but "spend 15 minutes on the report instead of 3 hours."
- [ ] **Benefits are addressed to the reader.** The copy lives in the reader's world, not the company's world. Not "we use technology X," but "you get Y thanks to technology X."
- [ ] **No feature lists without context.** A list like "CRM, analytics, automation, integrations" says nothing. Each item should explain why it matters.
- [ ] **The benefit-to-cost ratio favors the reader.** "Cost" is any effort the reader must invest (signing up, filling out a form, waiting, paying). The copy should make it clear that the benefit outweighs the cost.

### Translation Formula

```
Feature: [what it is]
→ Benefit: [what it gives the user]
→ Proof: [why this is true]
```

### Examples

| Feature | Benefit |
|---|---|
| 1 TB SSD drive | Windows boots in 8 seconds. Enough space for 200,000 photos |
| 24/7 support | Something broke at 3 AM before a deadline? Message us — we reply within 5 minutes |
| Integration with QuickBooks | No manual data entry: orders automatically appear in your accounting |

## 5. Stop Words and Verbal Clutter

**Principle:** Stop words take up space but carry no meaning. They dilute the text and erode trust. Remove them and the copy becomes sharper.

### Checklist

- [ ] **No filler introductions.** Remove: "at the present time," "as of today," "as is well known," "it's no secret that," "it is worth noting," "it should be emphasized," "it is important to understand," "it should be taken into account."
- [ ] **No bureaucratic language.** "Implement" → "do." "Utilize" → "use." "In order to" → "to." "In the framework of" → cut or rephrase. "With respect to" → cut.
- [ ] **No nominalizations (where a verb works better).** "The implementation of delivery" → "we deliver." "The provision of security" → "we protect." "The execution of analysis" → "we analyze."
- [ ] **No empty intensifiers.** "Very," "truly," "really," "literally," "absolutely" — if the meaning doesn't change without them, remove them.
- [ ] **No pronoun clutter.** Excess "our," "your," "its," "this," "said" — remove those that the sentence doesn't need.
- [ ] **No advertising clichés.** "Dynamically growing company," "individual approach," "team of professionals," "in the best traditions," "wide range of services," "flexible discount system," "one-stop shop," "turnkey solution" — all verbal junk unless backed by facts.

## 6. Structure and Layout

**Principle:** A landing page is not a wall of text. It's a sequence of blocks, each with its own job. Block order goes from most important to least important.

### Checklist

- [ ] **The hero section immediately answers 3 questions:** What is this? Who is it for? What's the result? If after reading the first screen it's unclear what's being sold — that's a problem.
- [ ] **No walls of text.** Each paragraph: one idea. Paragraphs no longer than 3–4 lines on screen.
- [ ] **There's a narrative logic.** Blocks follow an order: problem → solution → how it works → proof (testimonials, numbers) → call to action. Not necessarily in this exact order, but there must be a clear logic.
- [ ] **The CTA is specific.** Not "Submit a request," but "Get a cost estimate in 30 minutes" or "Try free for 14 days."
- [ ] **Image captions are informative.** If there are images, the caption shouldn't say "Our team" but "Ivan and Maria — lead engineers who have designed 50+ ventilation systems."
- [ ] **No duplication.** The same idea isn't repeated across different blocks in different words.

---

## 7. Honesty and Respect for the Reader

**Principle:** Copy should not manipulate, pressure, scare, or deceive. The reader is an adult who will make their own decision if given enough information.

### Checklist

- [ ] **No manufactured urgency.** "Today only!", "Only 3 spots left!", "Price goes up in 2 hours!" — if it's not true, remove it. If it is true, back it up with a fact.
- [ ] **No manipulative questions.** "Tired of accounting problems?", "Want to earn more?" — this is cheap rhetoric. Replace with a statement or a concrete scenario.
- [ ] **No lies.** "Guaranteed results," "100% satisfied clients," "Absolute security" — if you can't guarantee it, don't write it.
- [ ] **No fear-based pressure.** "Without our product, you'll lose customers" is manipulation. "With our product, reps follow up on time, and conversion grows by 15%" is a fact.
- [ ] **Fine print doesn't contradict the headline.** If the headline says "Free" but the asterisk reads "*with purchases over $500" — that's dishonest. Write upfront: "Free shipping on orders over $500."
- [ ] **Testimonials look real.** A real testimonial contains specifics: name, company, outcome. "Great company, highly recommend!" is either fake or useless.

## Agent Operating Principles

1. **Don't rewrite the entire copy.** Your job is to find issues and suggest targeted fixes. The author decides what to change.
2. **Preserve the brand voice.** If the landing page is informal, don't suggest dry corporate phrasing. If it's formal, don't suggest slang.
3. **Don't over-dry.** Infostyle doesn't mean "delete all adjectives." It means "replace empty words with meaningful ones." Vivid metaphors, stories, and examples are good.
4. **Facts > opinions.** If you don't have exact numbers, mark the spot as [NEEDS SPECIFICS] and suggest what information to request from the site owner.
5. **Prioritize.** The first screen (hero section) matters most. Headlines matter more than body text. CTA matters more than the footer.
